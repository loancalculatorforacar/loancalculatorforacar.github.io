How Car Loan Payments Are Calculated (Formula and Example)

By Mansoor Khan · Published November 9, 2026 · Updated November 9, 2026

Your car loan payment looks like one number, but it is two things added together: repayment of the money you borrowed (principal) and the lender’s charge for lending it (interest). Understanding the split helps you borrow smarter.

Step 1: Work out the amount you borrow

Start with the vehicle price. Add sales tax and fees. Subtract your down payment and trade-in. The result is the principal, P.

Step 2: Apply the formula

M = P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)−n)

Here r is your APR divided by 12 and n is the number of months.

Worked example

You borrow $26,000 at 6.5% APR for 60 months. The monthly rate is 0.065 ÷ 12 = 0.005417. The formula gives a payment of about $509. Over five years you pay roughly $30,540, so interest costs about $4,540.

Why early payments are mostly interest

Interest is charged on the balance you still owe. At the start the balance is highest, so most of each payment goes to interest. As the balance falls, more goes to principal. Paying extra early in the loan saves the most.

What changes the payment most

  • Term: longer terms lower the payment but raise total interest.
  • Rate: one extra percentage point adds hundreds of dollars over a five-year loan.
  • Down payment: borrowing less reduces everything else.
